Index Funds for Beginners: What They Are and How to Start

By YOUR NAME · Nov 20, 2026 · 7 min read

Picking individual stocks is hard, even for professionals. Index funds offer a simpler path: instead of trying to beat the market, you buy a small piece of the whole market.

What is an index fund?

An index fund is a mutual fund or exchange-traded fund (ETF) designed to track a market index, such as the S&P 500, which covers around 500 large U.S. companies. When you buy one share of the fund, you indirectly own small slices of every company in the index.

Why beginners like them

  • Low cost: Because there's no team picking stocks, expense ratios are often well under 0.20% a year.
  • Diversification: One purchase spreads your money across hundreds or thousands of companies.
  • Simplicity: No need to research individual businesses.
  • Track record: Studies such as the SPIVA reports have repeatedly found that most actively managed funds underperform their benchmark over long periods.

Why fees matter

Suppose you invest $10,000 and earn an average 7% a year before fees for 30 years. A 0.10% fee leaves roughly $73,000. A 1.00% fee leaves roughly $57,000. The difference of about $16,000 comes only from costs. This is an illustration, not a prediction of returns.

The risks

Index funds are not risk-free. If the market falls 30%, your fund will fall about 30% too. They suit money you won't need for at least five years. Past performance doesn't guarantee future results.

How to start

1. Choose an account

If your employer offers a retirement plan with a match, start there. Otherwise consider an IRA or a regular brokerage account. Rules and limits depend on your country, so check official sources.

2. Pick a broad fund

Look for a total-market or S&P 500 index fund with a low expense ratio and no sales load.

3. Invest regularly

Investing a fixed amount each month, known as dollar-cost averaging, reduces the stress of trying to time the market.

4. Stay the course

Avoid checking prices daily or selling in a panic. Review your plan once or twice a year.

Bottom line

Index funds won't make you rich overnight, but low costs, wide diversification and patience make them a sensible foundation for many long-term investors.
